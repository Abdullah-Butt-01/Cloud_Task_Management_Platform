PROBLEM ANALYSTS ROADMAP

Rapid Problem-Spotting Checklist

1) Observe Daily Frustrations:
i) Look for people repeating the same complaints: delays, costs, lack of info, inconvenience

2) Identify Gaps in Access or Inclusion's:
i) Notice if certain groups lack access to basic services (healthcare, education, transportation, finance).

3) Watch for Waste or Inefficiency:
i) Food waste, unused books, idle park spots. Ask. How could this be repurposed or reallocated?

4) Spot Friction Points in Routine Processes?
i) Pinpoint steps that repeatedly slow down people (manual work, paperwork, unreliable communication)

5) Listen for Systematic or Structural Issues.
i) Lack of trust in institutions, unclear regulations, gender or caste based barriers.

6) Capture Emerging Trend Anxieties:
i) Digital Privacy, Environmental Degradation

7) Look for Under-served Groups.
i) Identify age groups, income levels, or professions that lack certain products or services.

8) Scan Online Communities & Social Media:
i) Notice Pattern of complaints

In-Depth Problem Validation (whether it's worth building a solution around or not)
1) Severity of Pain
  ⅰ) People spend excessive time, money or emotional energy
  Signals: Frequent complaints,
2) Frequency
  ⅰ) Large no. of people experience the pain repeatedly.
  Signals: Lines at clinic, crowded waiting rooms, long queues at counters
3) Willingness to Pay (WTP)
  ⅰ) People already spend money on makeshift.
4) Lack of effective alternatives
  ⅰ) Existing solutions are expensive, unreliable, or unavailable locally
5) Social Impact & Urgency
  ⅰ) Will addressing the problem could drastically improve quality of life or safety.
6) Scalebiliti Potential
  ⅰ) The problem exists in multiple neighborhoods, cities or regions.
7) Ease of Early Adoption
  ⅰ) A minimum viable product or service (MVP or MVS) can be tested quickly to gather feedback.

Ideation: Generating Solution Concepts

1) Jobs-To-Be-Done (JBTD) Lens
    i) Ask: What do customers hire this product/service to do?

2) SCAMPER Technique:
    * Substitute: Replace a manual process with technology
    * Combine: Merge two services
    * Adapt: Take a successful model from another city
    * Modify: Increase a key feature
    * Put to another Use:
        * Eliminate: Remove unnecessary middleman
        * Reverse: Flip how service is delivered
        * Rearrange
3) Construct a list of Possible Solutions

4) Customer Interviews:
    i) Conduct quick 5-10 min chats with affected people.
    ii) Capture: Says, Thinks, Does, Feels.

Risk Assessment
1) Regulatory & Compliance Risk
What permits, licenses, or approvals does my solution need?
How stable they are?

2) Cultural & Behavioral Risk
Will people easily change their current habits?
Are there social or cultural norms that might resist my solution?

3) Operational Complexity Risk
How many moving parts are there?
Which processes are manual & prone to error?

4) Funding & Financial Risk
What is the total cost to start and run
What if actual usage is half?

5) Technology Adoption & Reliability Risk
Do the target users have devices, connectivity and skills?
How reliable is our technology in crisis scenarios?

6) Competitive Risk
Could established players replicate my idea quickly?
Are there cheaper or free alternatives available?

7) Scalability Limitations
As demand grows, will costs grow faster than revenue?
Are there bottlenecks that will choke expansion?

8) Timing & Market Readiness Risk
Is the market ready for the solution, or does infrastructure need to improve first?
Are there alternative trends that could obsolete the idea?

Prototyping and Testing Phase
1) Define Minimum Viable Concept (MVC)
2) Identify Pilot Audience
   i) Choose a small segment of users
   ii) Ask for honest feedback
3) Collect Data and Feedback
4) Iterate: Make changes quickly if required, ask the same group to validate improvements.
5) Measure Social Impact
6) Track all revenues

Scaling Strategy
A. Resource Mapping for Scale
1) Capital Needs: i) List all equipments, technology, budget and fee
 ii) Estimate how much capital is required
2) Talent & Roles: i) Identify new roles (Manager, Supervisor, Customer Care)
 ii) Document skills & experience required
3) Systems & Processes: i) SOPs
4) Partnership & Network: Formal agreements with other institutions required

Sustaining and Evolving
1) Monitor Emergent Issues Internally
2) Continuously Scan for External Changes
  i) Stay alert for policy shifts, competitor moves, or tech advancements.
  ii) Adjust your model as needed
3) Customer Feedback Loop
  i) Ask for any improvements or pain points
4) Brand & Community Building
  i) Sponsor Local events
  ii) Highlight Social Proofs
  ii) Create user content campaigns

Warnings & Common Pitfalls
1) Overlooking Root Causes leads to Surface - Fix Mistakes
i) Always verify root causes thoroughly
ii) Ensure your solution addresses true pain, not symptoms
2) Cherry Picking Anecdotes Ignoring Broader Data
i) Look for Broader patterns, not isolated cases
3) Underestimating Cultural & Behavioral Resistance
4) Scaling too fast without Operational Foundations
5) Ignoring Regulatory Shifts
6) Focusing more on technology instead of User Needs
i) Deliver the simplest possible solution that solves the core need, add advance features only after demand rises
7) Neglecting Cash Flow
i) Maintain a minimum of 3 months' operating cash for each segment.
8) Overreliance on one customer segment or Revenue Stream
i) Build at least 2 revenue pillars.
9) Misreading Local Variations as Universal Truths
i) Treat each new region as a fresh MVP
10) Failing to build a Learning & Feedback Culture
